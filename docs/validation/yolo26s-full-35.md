# YOLO26s-pose: full-video run at 35 streams

25 November 2026. An isolated benchmark build used the unchanged YOLO11x table/object detector and YOLO26s-pose as the person and pose model; the two original person-model calls were replaced by the pose result using [the experimental patch](pose-model-benchmark.patch). The old person-detector session was still loaded and was called during warm-up, so VRAM usage is not representative of removing that session. GPU concurrency 10; one video per independent stream; target 1 analytics FPS per stream.

All **35 streams processed 211 frames**, 7,385 in total, in **210.105 s** (35.15 analytics FPS system-wide). There were **0 late results, 0 dropped frames and no reported stream errors**. The mean across streams of their reported p95 result latency was 190 ms; their reported p95 GPU-queue waits averaged 115 ms. These are averages of per-stream p95 values, not a pooled p95. [Raw snapshots](yolo26s-full-35.json).

The host-wide GPU telemetry began approximately 14 s after the run started and covered the remaining 195 one-second samples: average utilization **35.5%**, peak **38%**, peak VRAM **7,416 MiB**. It can include other host activity. [Telemetry](yolo26s-full-35-gpu.csv). Sample-video frame extraction on CPU was performed concurrently during part of this run. Raw WebSocket clients received video for the first 10 streams and discarded it; browser rendering and delivery of 35 full video streams were not measured.

This is a single full-duration run, not a proven maximum or quality check. Frame-level differences from the current models are documented in [the candidate report](pose-model-yolo26s.md), and there is no independent quality annotation. At completion the measurement client wrote the complete data and then exited with a WebSocket-close error; the saved `run-end`, all 35 final stream metrics, and complete 211-frame counts were checked. The client was corrected afterward to ignore an error following successful completion.
